# Ledgerly product strategy: next two quarters

## Context

Ledgerly grew on small businesses. SMB is most of our logos but a shrinking share of revenue, and SMB churn is above 3% per month. Mid-market accounts expand and churn less. Most SMBs choose finance software on their accountant's recommendation.

## Goals

1. **Move upmarket into mid-market.** Win and expand finance teams of 5–50 people. That means controls a controller can defend to an auditor: approvals with segregation of duties, permissions that match how the company is organised, and reporting across entities. Measured by mid-market new and expansion ARR.
2. **Reduce SMB churn by removing weekly toil.** SMB owners leave when Ledgerly feels like a chore. We will focus on the jobs they repeat every week: bank reconciliation, trustworthy bank feeds, and getting paid. Measured by SMB logo churn and time-to-reconcile.
3. **Strengthen the accountant partner channel.** External accountants and bookkeeping firms bring us SMB clients. We will make Ledgerly the easiest platform for them to work in, both on their clients' books and on their own practice. Measured by partner-sourced signups.

## Non-goals (these two quarters)

- **Large-enterprise security and compliance programmes.** We will not build bespoke SIEM streaming, direct bank host-to-host integrations or ERP connectors for single enterprise deals. We will take enterprise requests that also serve mid-market (basic SSO, audit history), not ones that only serve the top five logos.
- **Becoming a tax engine.** We will integrate with tax providers when we do this work, not build rate tables ourselves. Tax is not planned this half.
- **Cosmetic invoice customisation** beyond what customers' AP departments require to accept an invoice.
- **Expense management depth** (receipt OCR, mobile apps, card programmes). We will keep expenses good enough for approvals and stop there.
- Localisation, vertical-specific accounting and one-off integrations.

## Principles

- **Solve the underlying job, not the requested feature.** Many requests describe a solution. We group them by the need behind them and then pick the solution.
- **Controls before convenience upmarket.** If a mid-market controller cannot defend it to an auditor, it is not done.
- **Trust is the product.** Missing or duplicated financial data costs us more than a missing feature does. Reliability problems jump the queue.
- **Accountants are a multiplier.** When two options are otherwise equal, pick the one that helps an accountant serving many clients.
- **Revenue at risk counts, but it is not a veto.** Churn risk and ARR inform priority. A single loud account does not set the roadmap.
